# Entries

## Two dictionaries

JMdict lists its German, Dutch and French glosses as separate senses. Tango
lines them up with the English meanings where the sense counts match and
lists the rest per language. Which languages an entry shows is a switch per
language on the General page of Preferences, with "Show first" among the
enabled ones; an entry that has none of them falls back to English, or
whatever it has.

![The language switches](../../data/screenshots/languages.png)

Wadoku entries show the pitch accent beside the reading: ④ means the pitch
drops after the fourth mora, ⓪ is flat, and a graph under the reading draws
it over the moras. Search covers both dictionaries in the order set on the
Dictionaries page.

![A Wadoku entry with its pitch accent](../../data/screenshots/wadoku.png)

## Speech

A speaker button on the reading and on example sentences reads them aloud
through speech-dispatcher when it lists a Japanese voice (Open JTalk,
VOICEVOX behind it, whatever is configured). Nothing is bundled; without a
voice the buttons do not appear.

## Every detail JMdict carries

Everything JMdict says about an entry beyond its glosses: usage and field
tags as chips, dialects, notes in italics, where a loanword comes from
("from German: Arbeit"), what a sense is restricted to, and "See also" and
"Antonym" buttons that open the referenced entry. Under the readings, what
the file says about single forms ("猫脊: rarely used kanji form").

![パソコン with its abbreviation chip and a see-also link](../../data/screenshots/details.png)

## Elsewhere

The button above the entry opens it on Jisho, Wadoku, Japanese Wikipedia or
Wiktionary, and in Takoboto for JMdict entries. Takoboto links
(`takoboto://` and the web ones) open in Tango.
